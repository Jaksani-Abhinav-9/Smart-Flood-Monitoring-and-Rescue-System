FLOOD RESCUE SYSTEM - POST-FLOOD RECOVERY, SANITIZATION & WELL DISINFECTION
Home Rehabilitation, Shock Chlorination of Drinking Wells & Insurance Protocols
==============================================================================

1. STEP-BY-STEP RE-ENTRY PROTOCOL FOR FLOODED HOMES
Never re-enter a flooded property until official government clearance is given:
- Exterior Visual Survey: Look for exterior cracks in brickwork or concrete, foundation shifting, leaning walls, or sagging roof lines. If exterior walls are bowed, do not step inside.
- Smell Test: Sniff for gas before stepping inside. If you smell rotten eggs (LPG odor), evacuate immediately, keep away, and alert the Fire Department (101) or gas supplier.
- Electrical Power Safety: NEVER switch on the main electrical breaker or plug in any appliances until the home's entire electrical circuit and outlets have been tested, cleaned, and certified dry by a qualified electrician. Turning on wet switches causes flashover fires and fatal electrocution.

2. SHOCK CHLORINATION OF FLOODED DRINKING WELLS & BOREWELLS
When floodwaters inundate an open well, borewell, or underground sump, thousands of gallons of sewage-contaminated water enter the drinking source. Follow this official disinfection procedure before drinking:
- Step 1 (Pump Out Contaminated Water): Pump out muddy water until the well water becomes relatively clear.
- Step 2 (Calculate Bleaching Powder Dosage): Prepare fresh chlorine bleaching powder (approx. 30% available chlorine). Use 50 grams of bleaching powder for every 1,000 liters (1 cubic meter) of water in the well.
- Step 3 (Dissolve in Bucket): Mix the bleaching powder thoroughly with 10 liters of clean water in a plastic bucket until it forms a uniform slurry. Allow insoluble lime sediments to settle for 15 minutes.
- Step 4 (Apply to Well): Pour only the clear chlorinated liquid into the well, splashing the sides of the well walls to sanitize the masonry. Agitate the well water by lowering and raising a clean bucket repeatedly.
- Step 5 (Contact Time): Keep the well closed and undisturbed for at least 12 to 24 hours to allow chlorine to kill all bacteria, cysts, and viruses.
- Step 6 (Flushing): After 24 hours, pump the well water out through exterior taps until the strong chlorine odor reduces to a faint level. The water is now safe for domestic use (continue boiling for consumption).

3. INTERIOR DECONTAMINATION & MOLD ERADICATION
Mold begins growing inside damp walls, furniture, and fabrics within 24 to 48 hours of flooding:
- Wear Protective Gear: Wear an N95 mask, rubber gloves, and rubber boots when cleaning flooded rooms to prevent inhaling toxic mold spores.
- Scrubbing Solution: Mix 1 cup (240 ml) of standard unscented household chlorine bleach with 5 gallons (19 liters) of clean water. Scrub concrete floors, tiled walls, metal surfaces, and solid wood fixtures with stiff brushes. Let the solution stand on surfaces for 10 minutes before rinsing with clean water.
- Non-Salvageable Porous Items: Discard mattresses, upholstered sofas, foam cushions, carpets, padded rugs, pillows, and acoustic ceiling tiles that were submerged in floodwater. It is impossible to sanitize the inner foam and fabric, which will produce dangerous toxic black mold (Stachybotrys).
- Ventilation: Open all doors and windows to create cross-ventilation. Run fans (once electrical power is certified safe) to dry rooms completely.

4. INSURANCE, DISASTER COMPENSATION & DOCUMENT PRESERVATION
Before you throw away damaged furniture or shovel mud out of rooms:
- Photograph Everything: Take clear, timestamped photos and videos of every room, showing water lines on the walls, flooded appliances, damaged furniture, and cracked foundations.
- Record Make and Serial Numbers: Photograph the model and serial number tags on refrigerators, washing machines, inverters, and television sets before discarding them.
- Salvaging Wet Paper Documents: Do NOT try to peel apart wet sheets of paper, deeds, or photographs while wet (they will tear into pulp). Place wet documents and paper records flat into a home freezer inside sealed plastic bags. Freezing arrests mold growth and dries paper through sublimation, allowing pages to be separated cleanly later.
