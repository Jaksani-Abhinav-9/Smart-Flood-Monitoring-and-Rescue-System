==============================================================================
FLOOD RESCUE SYSTEM - COMPREHENSIVE SITUATIONAL SAFETY MANUAL
Actionable Emergency Instructions for Every Flood Scenario
==============================================================================

1. SITUATION: TRAPPED INSIDE A VEHICLE IN RISING FLOODWATER
- Immediate Action: If water is rising around your car, do not attempt to restart a stalled engine. Turn on hazard lights immediately, unbuckle your seatbelt, and roll down your side windows BEFORE electrical power fails. Water pressure against the exterior of doors makes them impossible to push open until water equalizes inside the vehicle.
- If Windows Won't Open: Do not try to kick through the windshield; modern windshields are laminated safety glass that will not shatter. Target the front or rear side windows. Use a dedicated emergency glass hammer or remove the seat headrest and forcefully jam one of its metal prongs into the lower bottom corner of the side window to shatter it.
- Sinking Vehicle Protocol: Once water covers the hood, climb out through the window onto the roof of the vehicle. Never remain seated inside an enclosed submerged car. From the car roof, evaluate if safe dry ground is within wading reach; if water is fast-moving, remain on the roof and signal for help unless the vehicle is being carried into deeper water.

2. SITUATION: TRAPPED IN A SINGLE-STORY OR GROUND-FLOOR RESIDENCE
- In a single-story building where water breaches the doorstep, do not remain in lower living areas. Immediately identify your highest sturdy furniture, kitchen countertop, or concrete loft.
- Attic Trap Danger: NEVER enter an enclosed attic crawlspace unless there is a pre-existing rooftop opening or roof skylight, or unless you have a heavy crowbar, axe, or sledgehammer in hand. As floodwaters rise to ceiling level, families become trapped against the underside of the roof deck with no air pocket and drown. If no roof hatch exists, move outdoors onto an elevated porch beam or boundary wall if safe from currents, or call 112/108 immediately.
- Gas and Power Shutoff: If you can reach the main breaker without touching water, flip the main power switch to 'OFF'. Turn the brass valve atop your LPG gas cylinder clockwise until fully shut, and disconnect the regulator hose to prevent gas leaks into the rising water.

3. SITUATION: CAUGHT OUTDOORS IN A SUDDEN FLASH FLOOD
- Flash floods develop within minutes of heavy cloudbursts, dam releases, or embankment breaches, with little or no advance warning.
- High Ground Immediate Movement: Move perpendicular to the flow of water towards higher elevation immediately. Do not stop to retrieve bicycles, motorcycles, bags, or belongings.
- Bridge Dangers: Stay off low bridges, culverts, and causeways. Rushing floodwaters exert tons of lateral hydrostatic pressure that can compromise concrete bridge pillars and wash out road approaches without visible warning.
- Caught in Strong Current: If swept off your feet by water, do not fight against the current. Roll onto your back into the defensive swimming position: keep your feet pointing downstream (with toes sticking out of the water) and your head pointed upstream. Use your feet to push off against rocks, debris, and submerged obstacles. Angle your body at a 45-degree angle to the current to ferry-glide toward the nearest riverbank or sturdy tree.

4. SITUATION: STRANDED ON A ROOFTOP OVERNIGHT IN HEAVY RAIN
- Exposure & Hypothermia: Rain, wind, and wet clothing rapidly cause hypothermia even in tropical climates when temperatures drop below 20°C (68°F). Wring out soaked clothing. Wrap everyone in waterproof plastic tarpaulins, raincoats, or dry garbage bags over clothing to trap body heat. Huddle closely together with children in the center of the group.
- Lightning Precautions: If a thunderstorm accompanies the flood, avoid standing at the highest open point of a flat roof. Do not hold metal poles, umbrella shafts, or television antennas. Crouch low on non-conductive materials (such as a rubber mat, plastic tarp, or wooden plank) with your feet close together.
- Secure Anchor: If the roof is sloped or tiled, tie a secure safety rope around the chimney, roof beam, or reinforced concrete pillar, and have everyone hold the line to prevent slipping in darkness.

5. SITUATION: RURAL, FARM & AGRICULTURAL INUNDATION
- Open Wells and Borewells: Floods submerge open agricultural wells, farm drainage ditches, and uncovered borewells, transforming flat fields into deadly drowning pits. Do not walk across flooded fields or farm paths unless following elevated bunds marked by tall trees.
- Livestock & Animals: Untie or unchain cows, buffaloes, goats, and working animals before water reaches shed height. Livestock are natural swimmers and will seek higher ground if freed, but will drown agonizingly if left tethered in flooded stalls. Never leave animals locked inside enclosed sheds.
- Mud and Adobe Houses (Kaccha Structures): Mud-brick walls and thatched structures rapidly absorb floodwater, lose load-bearing capacity, and collapse without warning. Evacuate mud structures immediately at the first sign of inundation; move to reinforced concrete schools, panchayat buildings, or temples.

6. SITUATION: POST-FLOOD RETURN & RE-ENTRY SAFETY
- Do not return to a flooded home until local authorities issue an official 'All Clear' declaration.
- Structural Inspection: Before stepping inside, inspect exterior walls for deep cracks, foundation shifting, warped door frames, or sagging roof lines. If walls are bowed or cracked, do not enter; the building may collapse.
- Gas Leak Check: Sniff carefully for the rotten-egg odor of LPG or natural gas before entering. If you smell gas or hear a hissing noise, do not enter, do not flip any electrical switches, and do not use matches or open flames. Evacuate the area and notify the gas distributor or fire service.
- Electrical Reconnection: NEVER turn the main breaker back on if the electrical panel, outlets, or wiring were submerged. All wiring and appliances must be thoroughly inspected and certified dry by a licensed electrician to prevent fatal shocks or electrical fires.
- Mold & Sanitization: Scrub all flooded floors, walls, and hard surfaces with a solution of 1 cup (240 ml) household chlorine bleach per 5 gallons (19 liters) of clean water to kill toxic mold, bacteria, and sewage pathogens. Discard soaked mattresses, upholstered furniture, carpets, and drywall that were submerged in floodwater.
