==============================================================================
FLOOD RESCUE SYSTEM - CITIZEN FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQ)
Direct Actionable Answers for High-Urgency Emergency Queries
==============================================================================

Q: How long does a rescue boat take to reach me after sending an SOS?
A: An SOS alert reaches the central rescue dashboard immediately with your GPS coordinates. However, physical boat arrival is NOT instant. Rescuers must launch boats, navigate blocked streets, and battle water currents. Typical arrival time is 15 to 45 minutes in urban areas, and 2 to 4 hours in cut-off rural sectors. Rescuers prioritize immediate life-or-death situations first. Stay in a safe, elevated location, conserve your phone battery, and blow 3 whistle blasts to signal.

Q: Can the rescue boat evacuate my whole family and our luggage at once?
A: Inflatable rescue boats (IRBs) carry strictly 6 to 10 persons per trip to prevent tipping or capsizing. Rescuers cannot evacuate an entire colony or multi-family house in one pass. They evacuate in prioritized batches (injured, infants, and elderly first) and return for the remaining adults on subsequent runs. You must leave heavy suitcases, furniture, and appliances behind; you are only permitted 1 small waterproof bag with essential medicines, IDs, and phone chargers.

Q: What should I do if water is rapidly entering my living room or bedroom?
A: Initiate vertical evacuation immediately: move to an upper floor or reinforced concrete rooftop. NEVER climb into an enclosed attic without a roof exit or an axe, as you can become trapped if water reaches ceiling level. Before water touches electric sockets, switch off your home's main circuit breaker (MCB) and shut the valve of your LPG cooking gas cylinder. Fill bottles and buckets with clean tap water before supply lines are compromised.

Q: Is tap water or well water safe to drink after a flood?
A: NO. Tap water and well water are almost always contaminated with raw sewage, chemical runoff, and bacteria during a flood. Never drink unboiled water. Bring water to a rolling boil for at least 1 full minute, or use 1 chlorine/aquatab tablet per 4 to 5 liters of water and wait 30 minutes before drinking. If water is muddy, let it settle with a piece of alum (phitkari) or filter through a clean cloth before boiling.

Q: Can I drive through 1 foot (30 cm) of water if I have an SUV or 4x4?
A: NO. Never drive into floodwaters ("Turn Around Don't Drown"). Just 6 inches (15 cm) of moving water knocks down pedestrians; 12 inches (30 cm) floats cars; and 18 to 24 inches sweeps away even heavy 4x4 SUVs and commercial trucks. Roadbeds beneath floodwater are often washed out, and culverts can collapse invisibly.

Q: What should I do if my car stalls in rising water?
A: Turn on hazard flashers, unbuckle seatbelts, and roll down the windows immediately before the electrical system fails. Water pressure against the doors will prevent them from opening until the water level inside matches the outside. If windows will not roll down, use the metal prongs of your headrest to shatter the corner of a side window. Climb out onto the roof of the car and signal for rescue.

Q: What does blowing a whistle 3 times mean during a disaster?
A: Three short, sharp blasts on a whistle (blast - blast - blast) is the international universal distress signal. Rescuers and search teams are trained to listen for sets of three sounds. Whistles pierce through wind, torrential rain, and boat engine noise far better than the human voice, while saving your physical stamina.

Q: How do I handle someone who is unconscious after being pulled from floodwater?
A: Drowning causes respiratory arrest (oxygen starvation). Lay the victim flat on a firm dry surface. Call 108 immediately. Tilt the head back, lift the chin, and give 2 to 5 rescue breaths first, watching for chest rise. Then start chest compressions: 30 hard and fast compressions (100 to 120 per minute in the center of the chest) followed by 2 rescue breaths. Continue until the person breathes, an AED arrives, or paramedics take over.

Q: What should I do if someone is bitten by a snake during a flood?
A: Keep the victim calm and completely still; movement speeds up venom circulation. Keep the bitten arm or leg positioned below heart level. Immobilize the limb with a stick or splint and wrap a firm crepe bandage. NEVER cut the wound, never suck the venom, never apply ice, and never tie a tourniquet. Call 108 immediately or transport the patient to Kamareddy District Hospital or the nearest Community Health Centre for polyvalent Anti-Snake Venom (ASV).

Q: How can I keep my phone battery alive for 48 to 72 hours during a blackout?
A: Turn on Extreme Battery Saver mode immediately; reduce screen brightness to minimum; switch the display to grayscale; turn off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, vibration, and background app refresh; close all social media apps. Keep the phone in a sealed waterproof zip-lock bag (touchscreens work through plastic). Use SMS instead of phone calls because SMS requires minimal network signal and automatically sends when a tower connection flickers.

Q: What should I do if I see a fallen power cable near water?
A: Treat all fallen wires and standing water near utility poles as live and deadly. Maintain at least a 30-foot (10-meter) safe distance. If you feel a tingling sensation in your legs, DO NOT take normal steps; stop, turn around, and hop away with both feet held together or shuffle your feet without lifting them off the ground. Report the fallen line to the electricity board or 112 immediately.

Q: What are the nearest emergency shelters in Kamareddy?
A: The main government-designated flood relief shelters in Kamareddy are: 1) Kamareddy Government High School & Junior College (Station Road, capacity 450), 2) Government Arts & Science Degree College (Nizamabad Road, capacity 600), 3) Collectorate Community Hall & Indoor Stadium (Housing Board Colony, capacity 350), 4) Municipal Town Hall (Gandhi Chowk, capacity 200). Call 1077 (District Control) for current occupancy and transport assistance.
