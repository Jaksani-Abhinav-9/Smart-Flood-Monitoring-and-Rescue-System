==============================================================================
FLOOD RESCUE SYSTEM - VULNERABLE POPULATIONS & SPECIAL MEDICAL CARE
Emergency Protocols for Infants, Pregnant Women, Elderly & Chronic Conditions
==============================================================================

1. INFANTS AND NEWBORNS IN FLOOD ZONES
- Thermal Protection: Infants cannot regulate their body temperature efficiently and will develop hypothermia rapidly in wet clothing. Keep babies dressed in layers with a warm knit cap, wrapped in dry towels inside a waterproof plastic outer shell. Practice skin-to-skin contact (kangaroo care) beneath an adult's dry jacket to transfer maternal body heat.
- Feeding & Formula Safety: Breastfeeding is the safest infant feeding method during floods because breast milk requires no clean water, clean bottles, or electricity, and delivers protective antibodies against waterborne diarrheal disease. If using infant formula: never mix powder with unboiled floodwater; use only sealed bottled water or water boiled vigorously for 1 minute. Clean feeding cups and nipples with boiling water. Never dilute formula to make it last longer; doing so causes fatal electrolyte imbalances.
- Diapering & Sanitation: Change wet diapers promptly to prevent skin breakdown and infection. If disposable diapers run out, use clean cotton cloths, boiling and drying them when possible. Keep infant umbilical cord stumps clean and dry.

2. PREGNANT WOMEN & EMERGENCY MATERNITY CARE
- Priority Evacuation Triage: Pregnant women in their second and third trimesters qualify for Priority 1 (RED) evacuation triage. Flood stress, dehydration, and cold exposure can trigger premature labor or pre-eclampsia. Notify rescue teams immediately when an SOS is sent by stating gestational age in weeks.
- Warning Signs Needing Immediate Medical Extraction: Active uterine contractions occurring every 5 minutes, rupture of membranes (amniotic fluid leakage), vaginal bleeding, severe unremitting headache with blurred vision, or decreased fetal movements. Call 108 or 102 immediately.
- Emergency Delivery Kit (If Trapped): If delivery is imminent before rescue arrives: prepare clean dry towels, sterile cotton string/twine to tie the umbilical cord in two places, a new unused razor blade boiled for 5 minutes, clean boiled water, and soap. Do NOT pull on the umbilical cord; allow the placenta to deliver naturally. Keep both mother and newborn warm and sheltered until medical transport arrives.

3. DIALYSIS PATIENTS (WHEN SESSIONS ARE DELAYED BY FLOODS)
When floodwaters block transport to dialysis centers, end-stage renal disease patients face life-threatening fluid overload and hyperkalemia (high blood potassium):
- Extreme Fluid Restriction: Limit total fluid intake (water, tea, soup, ice) to no more than 500 ml (approx. 2 small glasses) per 24-hour period, plus urine output if any.
- Strict Low-Potassium Diet: Strictly avoid foods high in potassium such as bananas, oranges, coconut water, tomatoes, potatoes, spinach, dates, and salt substitutes (potassium chloride). High potassium levels can cause sudden cardiac arrest.
- Safe Low-Potassium Options: Eat boiled white rice, white bread, apples, cabbage, cucumber, and refined flour (maida) products.
- Emergency Medical Transport: Contact DEOC at 1077 or 108 stating, "End-stage renal patient missing scheduled dialysis, urgent hospital evacuation required."

4. DIABETES MANAGEMENT & OFF-GRID INSULIN STORAGE
- Insulin Temperature Management: Unopened insulin vials maintain potency at room temperature (below 25°C / 77°F) for up to 28 days without refrigeration. If ambient temperatures are high during a summer flood, wrap insulin vials in a damp cotton cloth and place them inside an unglazed earthen clay pot (matka) or insulated vacuum thermos; evaporative cooling keeps the inside several degrees cooler than ambient air. NEVER allow insulin to freeze or float in raw floodwater.
- Hypoglycemia Preparedness: Keep quick-acting carbohydrates (sugar packets, glucose biscuits, jaggery/gud, hard candy) in a sealed waterproof pouch in your go-bag. If shivering, sweating, trembling, or confusion occurs, consume 15 grams of glucose immediately.
- Foot Protection: Diabetics with neuropathy must NEVER walk barefoot or in thin sandals through floodwaters. A tiny unseen nail or puncture wound can rapidly lead to severe gangrene and amputation due to wet bacterial contamination.

5. PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES & MOBILITY CHALLENGES
- Triage Identification: Inform rescue dispatchers of specific physical or sensory impairments (wheelchair user, blind, deaf, non-verbal).
- Wheelchairs: Inflatable boats cannot safely transport heavy electric wheelchairs. Secure the person's manual wheelchair, crutches, or cane. If manual transfer is necessary, use a two-person seat carry or sturdy canvas sheet.
- Sensory Impairments: For hearing-impaired individuals who cannot hear warning sirens or boat engines, maintain visual contact and flash lights. Use written notes or visual cards for evacuation steps. For visually impaired individuals, assign a designated guide and keep service animals harnessed.
