==============================================================================
FLOOD RESCUE SYSTEM - EMERGENCY FOOD, COOKING & NUTRITION PROTOCOLS
Safe Ration Management, Food Contamination Rules & Sanitization
==============================================================================

1. IDENTIFYING CONTAMINATED FOOD (WHAT TO DISCARD IMMEDIATELY)
Floodwater carries lethal bacterial sewage and toxic industrial residues. Follow the golden rule: "When in doubt, throw it out."
- Throw Away Immediately: Any fresh vegetables, fruits, cooked dishes, grains, flour, sugar, or spices that came into direct contact with floodwaters. Throw away all food packaged in plastic wrap, paperboard cartons, screw-top jars, or cardboard boxes that were submerged; water seeps through screw caps and crimped seams even if packages appear sealed.
- Canned Foods Safety Protocol: Metal commercially sealed food cans that were submerged in floodwater can be salvaged ONLY if the can is undamaged (no dents, no swelling, no leaks, and no rust). Remove the paper label (it harbors bacteria). Scrub the outside of the can with soap and clean water, then immerse the can in a sanitizing solution of 1 tablespoon of household chlorine bleach per 4 liters of clean water for 2 minutes. Allow the can to air dry completely before opening.
- Perishable Foods in Refrigerators during Blackouts: A fully closed refrigerator keeps food safely cold for up to 4 hours without power. A fully packed closed freezer keeps temperature for 48 hours (24 hours if half full). If the temperature rises above 4°C (40°F) for more than 2 hours, discard meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, and leftovers.

2. HIGH-CALORIE EMERGENCY FOODS (NO COOKING OR WATER REQUIRED)
During floods when cooking gas is disconnected and clean water is limited, maintain nutrition using ready-to-eat, high-density shelf-stable rations:
- Flattened Rice (Poha / Avalakki): Can be softened with a small amount of clean water or consumed dry with jaggery.
- Roasted Bengal Gram (Chana / Phutana): Excellent source of protein and complex carbohydrates; non-perishable and ready to eat.
- Peanut Chikki / Gajak & Jaggery (Gud): Provides dense calories, quick glucose, iron, and sustained stamina during cold and physical stress.
- Dry Fruits, Dates & Nuts: Almonds, walnuts, cashews, raisins, and dried dates provide essential fats, vitamins, and energy in compact volume.
- Glucose Biscuits & Energy Bars: High-calorie, packaged, lightweight, and easily distributed to children and seniors.

3. EMERGENCY COOKING SAFETY RULES
- Ventilation Warning: NEVER use portable camping stoves, charcoal braziers (angithi), or kerosene burners inside enclosed, unventilated rooms or rooftops with closed tarps. Charcoal produces deadly carbon monoxide (CO) gas, an odorless, colorless gas that causes asphyxiation and death within minutes.
- Cooking Gas (LPG): Before igniting an LPG burner, check for gas leaks by smelling the air. If floodwaters moved the cylinder, check that the brass valve and regulator connection are tight. If you smell gas, turn the cylinder valve off immediately and do not strike a match or lighter.
- Fuel Conservation: When preparing hot porridge or tea, use flat-bottom pots with tight-fitting lids to minimize fuel consumption and cooking time.

4. THREE-BASIN DISHWASHING TECHNIQUE (OFF-GRID SANITATION)
If you must wash food utensils without running tap water, use the humanitarian three-basin method:
- Basin 1 (Wash): Warm water with biodegradable liquid detergent to scrub off food residues.
- Basin 2 (Rinse): Clean, clear water to rinse away all soap residue.
- Basin 3 (Sanitize): Clean water treated with 1 teaspoon of unscented chlorine bleach per 4 liters of water. Soak clean utensils for at least 1 minute, then place on a clean surface to air-dry. Never dry with dirty or damp towels.
