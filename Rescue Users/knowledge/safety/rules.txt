==============================================================================
FLOOD RESCUE SYSTEM - CITIZEN FLOOD SAFETY RULES & SITUATIONAL DIRECTIVES
Official Safety Guidelines for Citizens in All Flood Scenarios
==============================================================================

1. THE GOLDEN RULE OF FLOOD SAFETY: TURN AROUND, DON'T DROWN
Never drive, walk, or cycle through floodwaters, regardless of how calm the surface appears. It takes only 6 inches (15 cm) of swiftly moving water to knock an adult off their feet. Just 12 inches (30 cm) of moving water can float and carry away a passenger car, hatchback, or small sedan. A depth of 18 to 24 inches (45 to 60 cm) will sweep away heavy SUVs, vans, and commercial trucks. More than 50% of flood-related deaths occur when vehicles are driven into hazardous floodwaters. If your vehicle stalls in rising water, abandon it immediately if you can do so safely, and climb to higher ground.

2. INDOOR FLOOD SURVIVAL & VERTICAL EVACUATION
If water enters your home, initiate vertical evacuation immediately by moving to an upper floor or reinforced concrete rooftop. NEVER climb into a closed attic or false ceiling unless there is an accessible roof hatch or you bring an axe/hammer to break through to the open roof; hundreds of flood victims have drowned trapped inside enclosed attics as water reached ceiling level. Before water touches electrical outlets, turn off the main circuit breaker (MCB) and shut the main valve of your LPG cooking gas cylinder. Never touch electrical appliances, switches, or breaker panels while standing in water or if your hands or shoes are wet.

3. MOVING WATER & OPEN WADING HAZARDS
Avoid walking or wading through floodwaters at all costs. Floodwaters conceal severe unseen hazards including dislodged manhole covers, missing storm drain grates, washed-out roadbeds, sharp metal rods, broken glass, and submerged barbed wire. If you are forced to evacuate on foot through shallow standing water, always use a sturdy wooden pole, pipe, or umbrella shaft to probe the ground ahead of each step to test firmness, depth, and the absence of open pits. Never enter water flowing faster than normal walking pace.

4. ELECTRICAL AND ELECTROCUTION DANGERS
Treat all standing water near utility infrastructure as energized and lethal. Downed power cables, submerged transformer boxes, and flooded building switchgear can electrify standing water over a wide radius. If you feel a tingling sensation in your feet or legs while standing near water, DO NOT take normal strides; stop, turn around immediately, and hop away with both feet together or take tiny shuffling steps where both feet remain in continuous contact with each other, minimizing electrical potential difference. Maintain a minimum safe clearance of at least 30 feet (10 meters) from any downed power line or sagging cable, and report it instantly to the electricity control board or 112.

5. WATER CONTAMINATION & DISEASE PREVENTION
Floodwaters are heavily contaminated with raw municipal sewage, industrial chemical runoff, agricultural pesticides, animal carcasses, and decaying organic debris. Never drink floodwater or tap water from municipal lines that have been submerged. Direct contact with floodwater can cause leptospirosis (rat fever), cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, and severe skin infections. Wash your hands thoroughly with soap or alcohol sanitizer after any unavoidable contact with floodwaters. Keep open cuts, wounds, or abrasions completely covered with waterproof sterile dressings.

6. NIGHTTIME FLOOD SAFETY & BLACKOUT PROTOCOLS
During flood emergencies, power grids are frequently shut down to prevent mass electrocutions. In total darkness, use only battery-powered LED flashlights, headlamps, or emergency lanterns. NEVER use open flames such as candles, matches, oil lamps, or kerosene lanterns; ruptured underground gas pipelines, leaking domestic LPG cylinders, or spilled vehicle fuel floating on floodwaters create an extreme explosion hazard. Keep flashlights in battery-saver mode, and use intermittent flashes rather than leaving them continuously lit.

7. PROTECTING VULNERABLE INDIVIDUALS (CHILDREN, ELDERLY, BEDRIDDEN)
Prioritize the movement of infants, children, pregnant women, the elderly, and persons with mobility impairments to the highest and driest rooms first. For infants, secure waterproof warm clothing, a supply of ready-to-feed formula, clean drinking water, and diapers. For elderly and bedridden individuals, ensure a 7-day supply of vital medications (insulin, heart medication, hypertension pills), emergency medical prescriptions, and assistive devices are placed into sealed waterproof zip pouches. Tie or mark identification tags with names, emergency contacts, and blood groups on children's wrists or inner clothing.

8. WILDLIFE, REPTILE & SNAKE HAZARDS
As floodwaters inundate agricultural lands, drainage canals, and vegetation, venomous snakes (cobras, vipers, kraits), scorpions, and rodents lose their natural burrows and actively seek dry high ground, including home verandas, stairwells, rooftops, window ledges, and furniture. Always inspect stairs, door sills, and elevated surfaces with a flashlight before placing hands or feet there. Never reach blindly into storage cupboards, attics, or ceiling spaces. In the event of a snakebite, keep the patient calm, immobilize the affected limb below heart level, and call 108 emergency services immediately; do NOT attempt to cut, suck, or tie tight tourniquets on the wound.
